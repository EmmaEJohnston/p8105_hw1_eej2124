### Creating a data frame

``` r
set.seed(0)
problem_2_df = 
  tibble(
    random_sample = rnorm(10),
    vec_logical = random_sample > 0, 
    vec_character = character(length = 10),
    vec_factor = factor(c(length = 10), levels = 3)
  )
```

# Fix vector factor

### Taking the mean of each variable

``` r
round(mean(pull(problem_2_df, random_sample), 2))
```

``` r
round(mean(pull(problem_2_df, vec_factor), 2))
```

    ## Warning in mean.default(pull(problem_2_df, vec_factor), 2): argument is not
    ## numeric or logical: returning NA

    ## [1] NA

The mean of the random sample is 0. The mean of the logical vector is 0.
The mean of the character vector is NA. The mean of the factor vector is
NA. You cannot take the mean of the character nor factor vectors.

### Converting to numeric variables

``` r
as_numeric_logical = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_logical))

as_numeric_logical

as_numeric_character = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_character))

as_numeric_character

as_numeric_factor = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_factor))

as_numeric_factor
```

When you convert the logical variable to numeric, you get an output of
1s and 0s, which are being converted to numerical variables. However,
when you convert the character and factor vectors to numeric, you get an
output of ‘NA’. This shows why you cannot take the mean of these
variables, R does not understand them as numeric.
