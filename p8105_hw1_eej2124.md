The mean of the random sample numeric variable is 0.36. The mean of the
logical vector is 0.5. The mean of the character vector is NA. The mean
of the factor vector is NA. We see a return of NA when we try to take
the mean of the character and factor variables because they are not
interpreted as numeric.

### Converting to numeric variables

``` r
as_numeric_logical = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_logical))

as_numeric_logical

as_numeric_character = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_character))

as_numeric_character

as_numeric_factor = 
  as.numeric(pull(problem_2_df, vec_factor))

as_numeric_factor
```

When you convert the logical variable to numeric, you get an output of
1s and 0s, indicating that the logical variables were converted to
numerical variables. You can take the mean of logical variables like
this one. However, when you try to convert the character variable to
numeric, you get an output of ‘NA’ because R does not understand them as
numeric.

``` r
round(mean(as_numeric_factor), 2) 
```

    ## [1] 1.9

When you convert the factor variable to numeric, you get a returns of
1s, 2s, and 3s. While you can take the mean of this, these numbers
represent the levels of the factor variable, not actual values, so
taking the mean is not very informative.
